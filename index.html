Team of 3 · I built the robots, the simulation, the autopilot loop and ROS 2

The Charles River Dam crossing rebuilt in Isaac Sim. ArduCopter flies the drone, ArduSub flies the ROV.

Built in one day for challenge R6, safer inspection of critical infrastructure. A Holybro X500 flown by real ArduCopter SITL runs a 37-waypoint mission along the canal walls and under the deck, where the bridge cuts GPS from 12 satellites to 7. Every waterline defect it flags becomes a task for a BlueROV2 Heavy on ArduSub, which dives with GPS off and navigates on a modelled DVL fed into the autopilot's EKF.

Both robots build one model of the structure: 2.96 million lidar points above the water and 1.58 million sonar points below it. The ROV never drifted more than 60 cm over a 200 m dive, and 4 of 4 flagged defects were inspected underwater. The demo is a replay of a recorded 21-topic ROS 2 bag, run live on stage from one Docker launch.

What is modelled and what is real

Real: ArduCopter and ArduSub flight code, MAVLink mission upload, the EKF, ROS 2 Jazzy, RViz2 and QGroundControl. Modelled: the GPS drop under the deck (the bridge lowers the satellite count), the Ouster OS0-128 lidar and 130-degree multibeam sonar as ray casts, murky water as Beer-Lambert attenuation plus silt, and a simulated thermal camera. Thirteen defects were planted as ground truth, nine above the water and four below, including a scour pit with exposed timber piles.

Concept for a USV startup · firmware-in-the-loop simulation and film

ArduRover 4.5 SITL drives the boat; Isaac Sim 6 path-traces the logged run.

A 4.2 m twin-waterjet USV runs an approved patrol route, compares its lidar map with yesterday's patrol and raises change alerts. On operator approval it stands off a contact, loiters on its jets, rejoins the route and berths itself on a solar charging float. The autopilot is the real Pixhawk firmware, ArduRover SITL, lock-stepped to my own physics over ArduPilot's JSON backend.

The physics is a Fossen 3-DOF manoeuvring model with jet spool-up lag, a reversing bucket, current and shallow-water drag, on a JONSWAP wind sea plus swell. Heave, pitch and roll come from the hull sampling that wave surface, and the rendered water uses the same wave field. Tuning cut median cross-track error from 1.64 m with a 3 m weave to 0.37 m.

Dell x NVIDIA GB10 · Hyperion x Grok Bot · Wajo x Anthropic

Twenty Nova Carters in an Isaac Sim warehouse, streaming live fleet telemetry.

Started at the Dell x NVIDIA GB10 hackathon: a teammate and I built the Isaac Sim warehouse of 20 Nova Carters and shipped the stack as six services in one Docker image on the GB10, where a local Qwen model on vLLM triages robot faults into tickets. I then added four agents (surveillance, war room, technician scheduler, Hungarian re-allocation) under one rule: code enforces, the model explains. Over 3 seeds they raised deliveries from 27.3 to 42.0, and every run replays to an identical event log.

For Wajo x Anthropic, a Claude dispatcher hands a confirmed fault to Fo, which places real phone calls, calendar invites and emails to an on-call technician while the fleet re-plans around the repair. Who it may contact is enforced in code: in a live run, when a technician said to send someone off the list, the agent refused. I also mounted a Franka arm on all twenty Carters as a mobile-manipulation testbed.

Startup study and pitch film · Isaac Sim path tracing + a custom water renderer

Path-traced in Isaac Sim; the water itself is a ray-marched medium composited from depth.

Divers clean zebra and quagga mussels out of power-plant and desalination intakes, and OSHA counted six suction-related diver deaths in 13 months. I built the case for sending a robot instead, then the film to pitch it. The robot and intake are rendered with Isaac Sim path tracing, MDL materials and procedural mussel-clump instancers, writing raw HDR and depth for every frame.

The water is my own compositor working from that depth: absorption, backscatter, ray-marched god rays and lamp beams, caustics, Snell's window and drifting particles. Profiling the render found that hiding instances far below the scene bloated the BVH and made every frame 24 s; sinking them into the wall instead brought it to 5.7 s.

The engineering, in four writeups

Co-op 01 / Sensors, perception hardware and the operator UI Every sensor on the vehicle, and the interface the pilot flies it with my ownership

Primary engineer, sensor stack and operator interface

This was my primary work: bringing up and owning every sensor the vehicle carries, and the interface the operator actually uses. Ping360 scanning sonar, three cameras, the VectorNav VN-100 IMU, depth, and the lighting.

A sensor that returns plausible-looking garbage is worse than one that fails, because nothing downstream flags it. The Ping360 drew a fake circular wall at 0.3 m on every beam while the real wall sat at 4 to 5 m. The bags looked valid, correct message counts, correct rates, non-zero ranges, so it cost two pool sessions before I traced it to first-echo detection in both the Python and C++ drivers and fixed it with a regression test.

The sensor stack in detail

Sonar. Ping360 mounted vertically, which means roughly 40% of beams hit the vehicle frame itself and return a tight 0.30 m. That is only separable when the vehicle is still, so the sector mask is part of the driver rather than a post-process. Raising the serial link to 2 Mbaud took a full 360-degree sweep from 23.3 s to 5.9 s.

IMU. I characterised the VN-100 and the autopilot IMU the estimate depends on: a 30-minute stationary log, 321k samples, Allan variance and Kalibr noise parameters. Gyro ARW came out at 0.26 deg/sqrt-hr. The first attempt was contaminated by a nearby speaker coupling into the MEMS accelerometer, visible only as tonal peaks in the FFT.

Cameras and lights. Three cameras streaming to the topside station, plus the lighting rig. Underwater the D455 stereo depth returned only 8 to 40% valid pixels, and flat-port refraction invalidates the factory calibration outright, so I derived an empirical correction that flattened a known-flat pool floor from 15 mm to 6 mm median plane residual.

Depth. The Bar30 read nothing however it was wired, because BARO_PROBE_EXT was a bitmask missing the bit for the MS5837, so the autopilot never probed the I2C bus for it.

Co-op 02 / Perception & machine learning Training a detector with no real data to train on sole engineer

Archenaux Robotics · sole engineer, simulation and ML pipeline · 26 commits

Imagery of the target objects is classified or does not exist. So I generated it. A seeded procedural world generator emits ground-truth labels by 3D-to-2D projection, which means every frame is labelled for free and any scene is reproducible from its seed.

The difficulty is deliberate: seven object types, three turbidity levels, current drift, marine snow, kelp and seabed distractors that a naive detector calls targets. The blocking problem was never recall, it was that the model flagged every barrel.

Why I publish the bad number too

The evaluation also showed 0% recall on three object types, because the real-photo training set contained no flat or torpedo-shaped examples. I would rather publish that than a clean chart. An unevaluated detector is worse than none, because someone trusts it.

Co-op 03 / Embedded & hardware bring-up Three failures that looked like software and were not my ownership

Archenaux Robotics · primary engineer, vehicle runtime and sensor bring-up · 52 commits

Archelon runs ArduSub in a vectored six-degree-of-freedom configuration. I own the runtime that ships to it and I did the sensor bring-up, which is mostly a story about failures that look like software and are not.

The depth sensor read nothing no matter how it was wired, because BARO_PROBE_EXT was a bitmask missing the bit for the MS5837, so the autopilot never probed the I2C bus for it. The joystick moved every topic on the dashboard and no thrusters, because ArduPilot silently discards RC override from any system ID other than the one it is told to trust. The companion computer browned out under load, which turned out to be rail sag down the tether rather than thermal throttling.

On numbers nobody can trace

None of those are findable in simulation. I wrote the power-budget analysis that justified swapping the companion computer, and it says plainly which of its figures are measured and which are estimated, because a number nobody can trace is worse than no number.

The runtime itself is a containerized stack shipping to the real vehicle: MAVLink router, MAVROS, cameras, joystick piloting, RViz and QGC as one multi-arch image, with a 14-second preflight GO / NO-GO check gating every dive and a boot service that survives power-on.

Co-op 04 / Simulation infrastructure A simulator other engineers can actually run my ownership

Archenaux Robotics · owner, shared simulation core · 97 commits across two repositories

A simulator only one engineer can start is not an asset. I own the core the other projects build on, and most of its value is that it runs on someone else's laptop on the first try.

Three containers on one network: autopilot software-in-the-loop, physics, and a ground control station with joystick passthrough, plus a dev container. GPU is optional with auto-detected vendor overrides, and a browser-based fallback means the interface works on headless, Wayland and WSL hosts. That last one resolved a display crash blocking a teammate.

About

I am an M.S. Robotics student at Northeastern, graduating April 2027, coming off a co-op where I led the engineering team on an eight-thruster inspection vehicle.

My work sits where perception hardware meets software. On the vehicle I owned the sensor stack, sonar, cameras, IMU, depth and lighting, plus the operator interface and the containerized runtime that ships to it. I also trained a detector on a world I generated, because imagery of the real targets does not exist.

Simulation is how I get there safely, not what I do instead. Every number on this page that says measured was taken off real hardware or a real dataset, and the bring-up behind it meant I2C bitmasks, refraction calibration and a power rail that could not hold under load.
